## Checkpoint 1: The Hardware Essentials (What to Buy & What to Avoid)

Connecting to a Department of Defense (DoD) or federal virtual environment requires physical two-factor smart card authentication (PKI). Here is what you need on your desk:

### 1. The Smart Card Reader
:::note[The "Zero-Driver" Golden Rule]
**Do NOT install third-party "CAC Enabler" software (like ancient PKard drivers from 2012).**  
Modern macOS (Sonoma, Sequoia, and newer) natively includes Apple's internal `SmartCardServices` engine. It recognizes Common Access Cards (CAC) and PIV credentials instantly upon plugging them in.
:::

* **Recommended USB-A Reader**: **[Identiv uTrust 2700 R](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07CV3B2PF)** or standard military-issued **[SCR3310v2](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B002N300MQ)**. These are battle-tested, standard-issue smart card readers that macOS supports natively.
* **Recommended USB-C Reader**: **[Belkin Smart Card Reader](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Belkin+USB-C+Smart+Card+Reader)** or **[Identiv uTrust 2700 R USB-C](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C75GNDGP)**. Plugs straight into your MacBook Pro or Air with zero adapters required.

### 2. The USB Adapter Trap
If you have a modern Mac laptop with only USB-C/Thunderbolt ports and a standard USB-A card reader:

## Checkpoint 2: Software Triage (The Right App vs. The Web Trap)

### Avoid the Browser / Web Client Trap!
When you receive your onboarding instructions, they might give you a web link (e.g., `client.wvd.azure.us` or `rdweb.wvd.microsoft.com`). Opening this in Safari, Chrome, or Edge allows you to see a remote desktop inside a browser tab.

**Do not rely on the browser client for daily work:**
* ❌ **No Direct Smart Card Signing**: You cannot digitally sign PDFs in Adobe Acrobat Reader inside the browser session.
* ❌ **Broken Audio Routing**: Teams meetings suffer from high latency and mic dropouts.
* ❌ **Clipboard Conflicts**: Copying and pasting large files or formatted text is severely crippled.

### The Real Client: Microsoft Windows App
Instead, install Microsoft's native macOS desktop client directly from Apple:

1. Open the **App Store** on your Mac (or click the direct link below).
2. Search for **Windows App** (published by *Microsoft Corporation*).  
   *(Historical Note: This application was formerly named "Microsoft Remote Desktop". The new Windows App is its official, modernized replacement).*
3. Click **Get** and install it.

### Step-by-Step Subscription

1. Click the **`+`** icon in the top toolbar (or click the prominent **Add Workspace** button).
2. **Enter Your Official Email**: Type in your enterprise email address:
   * **Army**: `first.m.last.mil@army.mil`
   * **Air Force / Navy / DoD**: Your respective `.mil` or agency domain.
3. **Auto-Discovery**: Windows App will contact Microsoft Entra ID (Azure AD) and look up your tenant's secure feed URL automatically.
4. **Authenticate with Your CAC**:
   * A Microsoft authentication window will appear.
   * Plug your CAC reader into your Mac and insert your physical CAC (gold chip facing up).
   * Select **Sign in with a smart card / certificate**.
   * Pick your **Authentication (PIV)** certificate (usually contains your name and EDIPI number).
   * Enter your 6-digit CAC PIN.
   * Approve any phone 2FA push notifications (via **[Microsoft Authenticator on iOS](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/microsoft-authenticator/id983156458)**) if required by your command.

Once verified, your remote desktop icon (e.g., *Army EITAAS AVD*, *Navy Desktop*, or *Virtual Workspace*) will populate cleanly on your dashboard!

## Checkpoint 4: The First Logon & Surviving Day One

Double-click your new desktop card to launch your remote session. You will be prompted for your CAC PIN once more as Windows boots up.

Once you are in, you are officially teleworking! However, you will immediately encounter three classic "Mac-to-Windows" friction points:

### Friction 1: "The screen is blurry, or everything is microscopic!"
By default, Windows App may launch with non-Retina scaling or restrict you to a single monitor:
* **The Quick Fix**: 
  1. Hover your cursor over the desktop card on your Windows App dashboard.
  2. Click the three dots (**`...`**) in the corner and select **Configure**.
  3. Check the box for **"Use custom settings"**.
  4. Toggle **"Optimize for Retina displays"** on, and check **"Use all monitors"** if you have external screens attached.
  *(See our full [Display Settings Guide](/avd-mac-tricks/reference/community-tips/#multi-monitor-support--retina-display-optimization) for screenshots).*

### Friction 2: "My AirPods aren't playing remote sound!"
If you launch your remote session and *then* connect your AirPods, Windows App leaves audio blasting through your Mac's internal speakers.
* **The Quick Fix for Today**: Always connect your AirPods to your Mac **before** double-clicking your remote desktop card.
* **The Permanent Fix**: Use **SoundSource** to dynamically force audio to your AirPods mid-session without restarting. See our comprehensive [AirPods Routing Guide](/avd-mac-tricks/reference/community-tips/#-audio--peripheral-passthrough-airpods-routing-with-soundsource).

### Friction 3: "Why doesn't Command + C copy anything?!"
On macOS, your thumb naturally rests on the <kbd>⌘ Command</kbd> key for <kbd>⌘C</kbd> (Copy) and <kbd>⌘V</kbd> (Paste). But Windows expects the <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> key. 

Worse: pressing <kbd>⌘ Command</kbd> by itself sends the **Windows key**, which opens the Windows Start Menu and pulls focus away from whatever you were typing!
